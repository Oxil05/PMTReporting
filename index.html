Module 01

The 8 Core Teaching Methods

Structured methodologies teachers employ to deliver content, build skills, and facilitate real-world understanding.

Method A

A. Lecture Method

An oral presentation by an expert designed to clarify information to a large group in a short period of time. Ideal for tackling specialized topics efficiently.

Oral Delivery Expert-Led Large Group
Method B

B. Demonstration / Performance

The teacher models a general principle using a concrete, real example first. Students then practice the exact skill based on what they observed.

Modeling Concrete Example Student Practice
Method C

C. Discussion Method

A free, two-way exchange between teacher and students for exploring attitudes, interpretations, questions, and opinions rather than just rigid facts.

Two-Way Exchange Attitudes & Opinions Interactive
Method D

D. Case Study

In-depth investigation of a single subject, group, organization, or event in its real-world context to analyze general principles and evaluate scenarios.

In-Depth Analysis Real-World Context Evaluation
Method E

E. Pairs or Small Group Work

Students collaborate in pairs or small groups on application and analysis problems. Complements larger course structures to foster active peer learning.

Peer Collaboration Problem Solving Group Work
Method F

F. Field Studies

Out-of-the-classroom activity presenting concepts in realistic settings. Qualitative data collection in natural environments (e.g., Field Trips).

Outdoor Activity Field Trip Qualitative Research
Method G

G. Simulation

Recreating conditions using mathematical/virtual models to test behaviors and predict outcomes in safe controlled environments (e.g. Virtual Labs, Simulation Games).

Controlled Model Virtual Lab Safe Testing
Method H

H. Role Playing

A dramatic approach where students enact learning episodes. Highly action-filled enactment through which participants depict real scenarios.

Dramatic Enactment Action-Filled Empathy Building
Module 02

Techniques of Teaching

The specific steps, styles, or artistic execution teachers utilize to accomplish immediate learning objectives.

What is a Teaching Technique?

Teaching techniques are individual steps and stylistic tricks teachers follow. It refers to technical skill, artistic execution, and the skill of manipulating educational devices to stimulate the learner's psychological processes toward effective learning reactions.

01

Question & Answer

Knowledge Focus

Stimulates critical thinking and checks knowledge retention through targeted questioning strategies.

02

Drill Method

Skill & Habits Focus

Reinforces repetitive practice to convert emerging skills into permanent automatic habits.

03

Appreciation

Attitude & Values Focus

Nurtures emotional connection, aesthetic appreciation, and positive student attitudes toward learning.

  • 1. The Subject Matter: Must align seamlessly with the nature of what is being taught.
  • 2. The Students: Must suit the age, nature, background, and maturity of the pupils.
  • 3. The Teacher: Based on the teacher’s personal ability, style, and pedagogical training.
  • 4. Time Allotted: Tailored to the realistic time limits available for the lesson.
  • Means to an End: A technique should never be the ultimate goal; it is a vehicle to achieve learning.
  • Judged by Effect: The success of a technique is determined solely by its learning outcome in context.
  • Primary Laws of Learning: Must tap into fundamental psychological laws of learning (Readiness, Exercise, Effect).
Module 03

Instructional Strategies

Why we teach this way: overarching methods, approaches, and frameworks that guide how content is introduced and mastered.

Clear & Explicit

Direct Instruction

Direct instruction uses explicit, teacher-led instruction to introduce new skills and concepts. The teacher models the skill, explains thinking processes, and guides students through structured practice.

Key Examples:
  • Modeling & Think-Alouds
  • Guided Practice & Feedback
  • Explicit Vocabulary Instruction
  • Mini-Lessons (10-15 mins)
Peer Interaction

Collaborative Learning

Tasks students with working together in teams to solve complex problems, develop creative ideas, and deepen understanding through meaningful dialogue.

Key Examples:
  • Think-Pair-Share Activities
  • Literature Circles & Jigsaw
  • Group Projects & Peer Review
Student-Driven Exploration

Inquiry-Based Learning

Encourages students to actively explore ideas, formulate questions, conduct research, and discover answers independently.

Key Examples:
  • Project-Based Learning (PBL)
  • Socratic Seminars & Debates
  • Hands-on Experiments & Research Tasks
Personalized Learning

Differentiated Instruction

Helps teachers meet diverse student learning needs by adjusting content, tasks, learning environments, or instructional delivery.

Key Examples:
  • Tiered Assignments
  • Flexible Grouping Strategies
  • Choice Boards & Scaffolded Texts
Continuous Checks

Assessment for Learning

Ongoing checks for understanding used to inform immediate next steps in instruction and evaluate student mastery.

Key Examples:
  • Exit Tickets & Quick Writes
  • Teacher-Student Conferences
  • 3-2-1 Prompt: 3 things learned, 2 interesting facts, 1 remaining question.

Fosters creative thinking and conceptualization.

💡

🤝

Encourage Participation

🎧

Develop Listening Skills

Enhances auditory and visual processing.

Classifications & Categories of Devices

01

Material Devices

Physical teaching tools used in instruction.

  • General Devices: Used across all subjects (blackboard, chalk, paper, books).
  • Special Devices: Used for specific subjects (lab equipment, compass, specialized tools).
02

Representations & Replicas

Physical scale models representing real objects.

  • Terrestrial Globes & World Maps
  • Anatomical / Architectural Models
  • Miniatures of real-world objects
03

Electronic & Digital Equipment

Modern technology tools and multimedia aids.

  • Computers, Laptops & iPads
  • Projectors & Interactive Screens
  • Films, Educational Videos & Slides

5 Criteria for Selecting Educational Devices

1 Supports Learning: Directly aids subject mastery and lesson goals.
2 Adapts to Students: Fits individual student needs and learning styles.
3 Balanced Quantity: Provides options without overwhelming the class.
4 Practical & Economical: Cost-effective and functional for real learning.
5 Ready to Use: Accessible, simple, and easily deployed in classroom settings.
